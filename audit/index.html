How it runs

Pay and send targets. Checkout collects payment; reply to the Stripe receipt with up to five domains and one line confirming you own or control them.
Authorisation checked first. No work starts until that statement is on record. If authorisation doesn't hold up, the payment is refunded.
Passive first, then careful active checks. DNS, certificate transparency, public exposure, banner and header collection. No exploitation, no brute force, no denial of service.
Report. Findings with the evidence that proves each one, what was checked and what could not be, and the fix in priority order.
Retest at day 30. Reply to the report thread when patches are in. The re-run is included.
What this is not. It is not a penetration test, not a compliance certificate, and not a guarantee that nothing else is wrong. It is a fast, honest check of the exposure class that attackers actually scan for, with the limits of the check stated in the report.

Authorised targets only. Assets must be yours or under written client authorisation. Authorisation is confirmed in writing before any active check runs.

Questions

Why is this so cheap?

Because the scan layer is tooling I already run and maintain — HostageLVX, surfacediff, and Fenrir are MIT-licensed and public. What you're paying for is the triage, the false-positive elimination, and a report a human can act on. It is fixed scope by design.

Do you exploit anything you find?

No. Findings are established with non-destructive evidence: the response, the certificate, the record, the banner. Anything that would require exploiting a vulnerability to prove is flagged for a follow-up engagement instead.

Can I send more than five domains?

Yes, five is included. Extra domains are $20 each, invoiced after you send the list, before work starts.

What if you find nothing?

You still get the report: what was checked, what was excluded and why, the current exposure inventory, and the retest. A clean result is a real result when the coverage is stated.
